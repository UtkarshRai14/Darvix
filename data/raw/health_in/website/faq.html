Frequently asked questions

Last updated: 12 June 2026

What is a pre-existing disease?

A pre-existing disease is any condition, ailment or injury that was diagnosed by a physician, or for which medical advice or treatment was received, within 36 months before the policy start date. Common examples are diabetes, hypertension and thyroid disorders.

Are pre-existing diseases covered?

Yes. Declared pre-existing diseases are covered after a waiting period of 36 months of continuous coverage. You must declare all pre-existing diseases honestly in the proposal form; non-disclosure can lead to claim rejection or policy cancellation.

What is the free-look period?

You get a free-look period of 30 days from the date you receive the policy document. If you are not satisfied, you can cancel the policy during this period and receive a refund of the premium after deduction of proportionate risk premium and medical check-up costs, if any.

What is the grace period for renewal?

The grace period is 30 days for annual, half-yearly and quarterly payment modes and 15 days for the monthly mode. Coverage continues without a break if the premium is paid within the grace period, but claims for hospitalisation during the grace period are not payable until the premium is received.

How does the no claim bonus work?

For every claim-free policy year, the sum insured increases by 10% of the base sum insured, up to a maximum of 50%. The no claim bonus is not reduced if a claim is made later.

Can I get tax benefits on the premium?

Premiums paid for health insurance are eligible for deduction under Section 80D of the Income Tax Act, as per prevailing tax laws: up to Rs. 25,000 a year for self, spouse and children (Rs. 50,000 if the eldest insured is a senior citizen), and an additional deduction for premiums paid for parents. Please consult your tax advisor for your specific situation.

Can I pay the premium monthly?

Yes. Premiums can be paid annually, half-yearly, quarterly or monthly. Monthly payments are collected through auto-debit or UPI AutoPay mandate. A small loading applies to non-annual modes: 2% for half-yearly, 3% for quarterly and 4% for monthly.

Do I need a medical test to buy a policy?

No pre-policy medical tests are needed for applicants up to age 45 who do not declare any pre-existing disease. Applicants above 45 years, or applicants who declare a pre-existing disease, may need a tele-medical interview or a pre-policy health check-up. The check-up is arranged at the company's cost. Senior Care always requires a pre-policy check-up.

Can I port my existing health policy to Nivaran?

Yes. You can port a health policy from another insurer at the time of renewal. Apply at least 45 days before your current policy's renewal date. Waiting periods already completed under your previous policy are credited up to the previous sum insured.

Is there any co-payment?

There is no co-payment under Nivaran Essential and Nivaran Family Floater for members below 60 years. Nivaran Senior Care has a 20% co-payment on every claim.

What is not covered?

Major exclusions include cosmetic or plastic surgery (unless needed due to an accident or burns), dental treatment unless arising from an accident and requiring hospitalisation, spectacles and contact lenses, self-inflicted injuries, treatment for alcohol or drug abuse, experimental treatments, and expenses during the first 30 days except accidents. Refer to the policy wording for the complete list.

Does the policy cover COVID-19 and other infectious diseases?

Yes. Hospitalisation for COVID-19 and other infectious diseases is covered like any other illness, subject to the initial 30-day waiting period and policy terms.

How do I buy a policy?

You can buy online on our website, through a Nivaran advisor on a phone call, or at a partner branch. A licensed advisor reviews your proposal form, and the policy is issued after underwriting and premium payment. Nivaran representatives will never ask for your OTP, card PIN or net-banking password.
